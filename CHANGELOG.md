# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Added
- Hovering the call's network icon shows the voice ping: the UDP round trip to the voice server in milliseconds, or "measuring…" until the first answer (#221). It is cleared when the call ends.

### Changed
- Paste image works on Linux (X11 and Wayland) and macOS as well as Windows, and a pasted image keeps its transparency (#146). The clipboard is read through `arboard` instead of hand-written Windows code; it is still read only when you ask.
- Secondary screens share one frame. Server settings, roles, emoji and sounds, Community, AutoMod, apps and integrations, bans, the audit log and a channel's own settings are pages of a full-window settings screen with a list of sections, like the user settings, instead of forms stacked over the conversation; "Server settings" in the server menu (and a right click on a server) opens it at the first page the account may use. Network recovery is the "Network" page of the user settings, translated, with short explanations and the technical ones under "Details". Smaller forms (a new channel or server, a member's roles, invites, reports, forwarding, events, rules and onboarding, the group panel, discovery, authorized apps) open as dialogs.
- Every settings screen and dialog has one title and one close cross; Esc closes what is on top: a popover, a dialog, the emoji/GIF/sticker window, a settings screen, then the inbox, alerts, search and pins. A new settings section opens at its top instead of where the previous one was scrolled to. Profile, Accounts and Privacy no longer repeat their own title and cross inside the page.
- Buttons are grey unless they are the main action of their section (Save, Create, Send…), which stays blurple; destructive confirmations stay red. Unavailable buttons fade their text as well as their fill. Role colours are picked from real colour squares, the chosen one ringed and ticked. Channels, roles and onboarding questions are reordered with up and down arrows; the first row's up arrow and the last row's down arrow are greyed out. Secondary text is lighter (#a5abb4) while staying apart from body text.
- The emoji, GIF and sticker window keeps one size and one place: the tabs and the cross no longer move when switching tabs, the Stickers tab is always there, and empty states and errors show inside the window. Going to another conversation closes it.
- Friends and group members keep the main action on the row (Message, Accept); removing a friend, declining, blocking, removing a member and handing over a group are in the row's `⋯` menu and still ask before anything is sent.
- The last message no longer touches the message box: a fixed 14 px gap stays between them with reactions, embeds and a multi-line draft.

### Fixed
- The sign-in requests made before a token exists (password login, QR ticket exchange, fingerprint, version check) share one cookie-and-size-limited transport (#147). The `/app` page fetch now sends the `discord.com` cookies it already kept, as a browser would, and none of them can read past its size limit.
- Muting a category, or setting its notifications to "Only @mentions" or "Nothing", now applies to the channels inside it, and a thread follows its channel and that channel's category (#148). Their new messages no longer light the channel, the server icon or the tray, and "hide muted channels" hides them.
- Pressing Reconnect after the client stopped reconnecting on its own no longer throws away your unsent drafts (#149): the text typed into each conversation carries over to the new connection. Messages still waiting in the outbox are not carried over yet.
- An audio device that hangs while closing (a dropped Bluetooth headset, a stalled driver) can no longer freeze the whole account when you leave or switch calls (#151). The client waits at most a few seconds for it, and closes devices and screen-capture threads off the network worker.
- On large accounts, unread dots and mention counts no longer vanish when the client trims its memory (#152). Users nothing refers to are now released first, and as soon as a member, channel or server is removed; read state is the last thing dropped, and if it is, the badges update instead of silently going stale.
- The channel list puts text channels (and forums) above voice and Stage channels in each category, each group by position, as Discord and the server settings do (#154). Servers that number voice and text positions separately were shown interleaved.
- A timed-out moderator is no longer offered server, channel and role management that Discord would refuse (#157). Server-level permissions now drop everything but viewing and reading history during a timeout, as channel permissions already did; administrators and the owner are unaffected.
- Emoji inside a spoiler stay hidden until the spoiler is uncovered (#158). They were drawn in plain view between the hidden words; a hidden one is now part of the grey box, and clicking it reveals the spoiler.
- A server icon or closed folder no longer shows an unread dot or mentions from channels you cannot open (#159). The total counts the same channels the sidebar and the inbox list, so a channel you lost access to cannot leave a badge nothing can clear.
- A masked link can no longer show one host and open another (#161). The host in brackets after a link and the address that is opened now come from the same URL parse, done the way a browser reads it (a backslash counts as `/`), and links that carry a user name or password are not opened.
- Messages from people you blocked or ignored no longer add to the mention count of the channel, the server icon or the tray (#162). Their notifications and mention-list entries were already hidden; the counters now follow the same rule.
- A channel with many emoji no longer re-reads each message once per emoji on every update (#163): the check of which emoji are still on screen now parses each message once per update.
- Server icons and your own avatar no longer fall back to letter circles after browsing many channels, and pictures that failed while the network was coming up are tried again (#165). The picture store now evicts what left the screen first; a network, 429 or 5xx failure is retried after 30 s, and a definite failure is forgotten once its picture leaves the screen.
- A download you started keeps going when its message scrolls away or you switch channels, and ends with its usual result notice (#167). Before, it was silently cancelled and the partial file deleted. Up to two downloads run at once on their own connections, so a large one no longer holds up avatars, emoji and previews.
- Avatars, server icons and stickers are no longer stored twice (#168). Their compressed original is decoded from memory and only the small sized copy is kept, so they stop taking space in the 256 MiB attachment cache. Each attachment download also scans the cache directory once instead of twice.
- In a call, a camera turned on late no longer stays black because earlier streams used up the six video slots (#171). A stream that received nothing for 5 s is released (it stops getting reports and keyframe requests too), and a full table makes room by releasing the longest-quiet stream.
- Attachment names can no longer disguise their type (#172). Bidi overrides and invisible format characters are removed, and trailing dots and spaces are trimmed, both in the saved file name and wherever the name is shown (message row, larger view, the edit list). Before, `photo` + right-to-left override + `gnp.exe` displayed as `photoexe.png` and saved as `photognp.exe`.
- Moderation, Stage and scheduled-event changes made while the account is not connected (the Gateway stopped and shows Reconnect) now say "not connected to Discord; reconnect and try again" (#173). Before, they reported rate limiting, or treated the lost answer as a refused login and signed the account out.
- AGENTS.md describes the workspace again (#185): one `fastcord-net` row, the AutoMod entry once, `fastcord-fake` and `platform` in the dependency block, `audio`/`video` arrows matching their manifests, and the bundled OpenH264 codec, VA-API and the Windows CAPTCHA web view named. `scripts/check_agents_map.py`, run in CI, fails when a crate has no row or an arrow no longer matches its manifest.
- Echo cancellation costs less than half as much CPU during a call (#194): 19 µs instead of 43 µs per 10 ms block, with identical cancellation in every echo test. It now uses real-valued SIMD transforms (`realfft`, already in the build through DeepFilterNet) and keeps the reference power as a running sum.
- Dependencies with published security fixes are updated: rustls 0.23.46, rustls-webpki 0.103.16, webpki-roots 1.0.11, tokio 1.54.1 and ctutils 0.4.6 (#201). CI now runs `cargo deny` (advisories, GPL-3.0-compatible licences, crate sources); the advisories that DAVE's pinned crypto and the bundled DeepFilterNet model still carry are listed in `deny.toml` with the reason each is accepted.
- Someone typing in a channel you are not looking at no longer rebuilds the client's whole view of the server (#153). Typing is still recorded everywhere, so switching channels shows who is typing at once, but only the open channel's typing redraws anything or arms the expiry timer.
- An animated emoji on screen no longer rebuilds the whole window for every frame (#155). Each emoji picks its own frame when the window redraws and asks for the next redraw when that frame ends, so a frame change is one redraw with no message, view rebuild or layout pass; an emoji scrolled out of view wakes nothing.
- The voice log names why end-to-end decryption refused incoming audio (#215): during a key transition, from a sender outside our group, unencrypted, or no key of the sender's epoch (or a replayed packet). All four were one "keys out of step" line, so a call that kept dropping audio could not say which one it was.
- The voice log no longer counts the end of every sentence as a playback underrun (#217). When nobody is speaking the mixer stops on purpose and the device buffer drains; that drain was logged as a dropout, so a normal conversation showed 5-15 "underruns" a minute. Only a buffer that runs dry while audio is still coming is counted now.
- A failed "Check connection" names the route it took instead of always saying packet recovery did not help (#218): with recovery Off it says the direct route failed and suggests Automatic, in Automatic without the helper it says the helper is not running, and through a proxy it says so.
- The network page no longer offers "Experimental UDP discovery (unverified)", and a saved `experimental_udp: true` is ignored (#223). With it on, a call on a filtered route spent its discovery budget on junk datagrams and closed after about 30 s although a plain join worked. The Voice and video section now says that the TLS bypass cannot reach a blocked voice server and that only a SOCKS5 proxy with UDP can.
- Incoming friend requests that Discord marks as strangers now offer a warning, Confirm acceptance and Cancel after its refusal (#211). Only an explicit confirmation sends `confirm_stranger_request: true`; ordinary accepts and new requests keep their existing bodies. Adding an already-pending username opens its incoming request, and withdrawn requests cannot be accepted from a stale confirmation.
- Participant voice volumes now save during slider and keyboard changes without requiring a release event (#219). A bounded background writer coalesces changes for 500 ms, flushes on release, account teardown and Quit, serializes file updates, and keeps recently changed rows. Loading preserves live changes; the call settings and member menu show save failures, and the account retention limit is visible.
- A blocked network is no longer a dead end without explanation (part of #218). When login fails with a timeout or reset before TLS completes, the notice points at Network recovery's packet helper, a proxy or a VPN. The Network recovery panel shows the active path (direct, TLS recovery, packet helper or proxy) and the recovery budget: probes in the running check, dials used in the last 10 minutes, and when new checks must wait.
- Discord now gets through a network that blackholes the `discord.com` name without a VPN: TLS recovery gained a strategy that works where plain writes and two-record splitting do not (part of #218). The plaintext ClientHello is rebuilt as four TLS records with the same handshake bytes, and each fragment goes out as its own write ending one byte before the next record boundary, so no TCP segment starts on a record header and a filter that parses per segment never sees a parseable hello. Recovery tries it second, right after a plain connection; on the reported route the anonymous `GET /api/v9/gateway` then answered HTTP 200 and later dials reused the confirmed strategy in about 100 ms. Handshake bytes, SNI, ALPN, certificate verification and the transcript are unchanged, no driver or administrator rights are involved, and the strategy catalog is now version 3, which expires previously saved route hints.
- On such a network only the first service used to recover: messages, avatars, attachments and voice stayed dead because the app needs five endpoints (login, REST, Gateway, CDN, voice) and recovery allows one campaign per endpoint at least 60 s apart, with 24 probe dials per ten minutes (part of #218). A confirmation now also applies to the other endpoints of the same address family for ten minutes, so the first shape found serves the whole session: measured on the reported route, after one campaign for login, REST, CDN and Gateway answered in 100-155 ms with no further probes. A reused shape that fails still records that endpoint's own demand, so nothing is assumed to work.
- A packet helper that exits no longer takes the whole client down with it. Its factory reports itself unavailable, recovery stops selecting it and returns to the ordinary TLS strategies, instead of failing every dial with `ConnectionAborted` until the app is restarted (part of #218).
- Late or lost incoming voice is concealed for up to 160 ms instead of 60 ms before the speaker goes silent, and the concealment fades out over that run, so a burst of jitter or undecryptable packets is a soft dip rather than a hard gap (part of #217). The voice log's PLC line now also names the current jitter-buffer target.
- Incoming Opus silence frames are no longer refused as end-to-end decryption failures (#215). DAVE sends the silence frame `F8 FF FE` unencrypted by design and davey passes it through, but the adapter rejected it, so every talkspurt's end produced dropped packets, concealment holes and a log full of "keys out of step". The voice log now also separates audio refused by the DAVE session from packets that failed the transport cipher, which shared one counter before. Not yet confirmed on a live call.
- A screen share or camera whose encoder runs slow (a game holding the GPU) now steps its rate down from 30 to 20 to 15 fps instead of starving, and climbs back once encoding is cheap again (part of #213). The rate changes at most every 2 seconds, and the share status line says "lowered to N fps, encoder busy" while it is down.
- A visible animated emoji no longer redraws the whole window 50 times a second (part of #212). The animation wakes once, when its earliest frame is due (still at most 50 times a second), so a 100 ms GIF costs 10 redraws a second instead of 50.
- Masked links whose address contains balanced parentheses, such as `[Rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_(language))`, now link to the whole address instead of stopping at the first `)` and leaving a stray `)` after the link (#205).
- Timestamps that name no real date, such as `2026-02-31`, are refused instead of rolling over into March; this affected scheduled event times and poll expiry (#204). RFC 3339 parsing and the Discord timestamp formatter now use the `time` crate instead of hand-written calendar code.
- The voice transport cipher uses the same `aes-gcm` 0.10 / `chacha20poly1305` 0.10 generation as openmls, so the binary carries one AEAD stack instead of two (about a dozen duplicate crates fewer) (#203).
- The Windows executable links the C runtime statically and no longer needs the Visual C++ Redistributable; a PC without it used to fail with "VCRUNTIME140.dll was not found" (#206).
- iced's image support no longer pulls in every `image` codec: the app hands iced decoded pixels only, so the rav1e AV1 encoder, EXR, TIFF, QOI and their dependencies (over a hundred crates) are gone from the build (#200).
- The macOS app declares `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription`, so the first microphone use shows the permission prompt instead of macOS terminating the app (#199).
- "Use external sounds" in the role and channel permission editors now sets its own permission (bit 45). It was mapped to bit 43, so ticking it granted Create Expressions instead and the two checkboxes moved together (#198).
- A message with a timestamp near year 9999, such as `<t:253402300799:F>`, no longer crashes the client for readers east of UTC (or west of it near year -9999); it shows as plain text (#196). Message, event and voice-log times all use the checked offset conversion.
- A microphone or speaker stream that dies while the device is still there (a format change in the sound control panel, spatial sound toggled, exclusive mode released, the audio service restarting) is reopened on its own after 0.5, 2 and 5 seconds instead of staying silent until the device is chosen again (#195). An unplugged device or denied access is not retried, and a device that keeps failing stays failed after three attempts.
- The echo canceller does no FFT work while nobody else is talking (part of #194). Once the reference has been silent for its whole history (about a third of a second) a step returns the microphone unchanged, bit for bit what the full computation gave; adaptation also divides once per frequency bin instead of once per bin and partition.
- Incoming voice now actually waits for the jitter buffer's target (#193). A talkspurt starts playing one target (60 ms at first, adapting between 40 and 160 ms) after its first packet; before, a speaker who had talked longer than the target was due on every pull, so ordinary arrival jitter was played as concealment instead of being waited for.
- A paused or finished voice message or inline audio/video attachment no longer keeps an audio stream and its engine thread running (#191). The output closes on pause or at the end and reopens on Play, so an idle client holds no audio callback stream after playing a clip.
- A call with more than four people talking no longer turns everyone's audio to buzzing (#189). The mixer holds 16 speakers instead of 4, and a full mixer only makes room by evicting someone who has been quiet for 100 ms; before, a fifth talker evicted a live speaker on every packet, so every voice was decoded from a fresh decoder each time.
- The result of a moderation action (kick, ban, timeout, nickname, move) is shown only in the menu of the member it was for (#188). Opening another member's menu while it ran used to show "Done." or the error there and wipe that menu's typed reason.
- The Events panel can no longer create the same scheduled event twice (#186). Close is disabled while a change is being saved, and reopening the panel keeps the request in flight, so a second Create waits and the first one's result is still shown.
- Closing the poll panel or switching channel while a poll is being sent no longer abandons the request (#184). The close button is disabled while sending and the send is awaited, so a poll Discord already created is not created again under a fresh nonce.
- Removing a friend and blocking someone now ask first (#183). Remove and Block in the friends list and on the profile card show Confirm and Cancel in place of the row's buttons; Decline and Cancel request stay one click.
- After a pause for new end-to-end keys or a reconnect, your voice resumes with live audio instead of first sending up to 100 ms recorded when the pause began (#182). Audio that waited for keys is dropped when media resumes, as stale video frames already were.
- Inbox buttons act on the conversation they were drawn for (#180). Open, Mark as read and Mute used the row's position, and the unread list re-sorts on every new message, so a click could acknowledge or mute another conversation; "Mark these as read" now covers exactly the rows it was shown over.
- Watching a stream copies each received video packet once less and grows the frame buffer once per frame instead of repeatedly (#179): the decrypted payload moves into the reassembly buffer (the retransmission header is stripped in place).
- In the server Emoji, Stickers and Sounds panel the tabs are disabled while an upload or a file pick is in progress (#178). Switching tabs mid-request used to clear the wrong tab's draft (so the same emoji could be uploaded twice) or put a picked image into the Sounds draft.
- A video sender that jumps its sequence numbers can no longer grow a stream's missing-packet table to about a million entries and make every received packet walk it (#177). The receive window from the oldest unplayed packet is now bounded at 2048 packets, like what it stores.
- In a call where many people have turned their camera on and off, a newly turned-on camera is still requested (#175). Cameras announced as off no longer keep one of the 16 remote-video slots, so the 17th camera used to be drawn as a tile that never received video.
- A call whose media path dies while the voice WebSocket stays up (roaming between networks, a dropped NAT mapping, a VPN switch) no longer stays "Connected" in silence forever (#174). After about 15 seconds with no UDP ping answer and no incoming audio on a path that had worked, the client reconnects with a fresh socket; through a SOCKS5 relay it ends the call so it can be rejoined.

### Removed
- The retired "Blocking bypass" setting is gone. Since the transport moved to `fastcord-net`, turning it on started nothing, yet the value was still stored, still displayed as on, and still accepted byedpi options such as `-I <address>`. The app no longer reads, writes or links `fastcord-bypass` at all; an old settings file loads normally and the stale `bypass` key disappears the next time preferences are saved.

## 0.1.0-alpha.7 — 2026-10-25

### Fixed
- Voice rooms no longer look empty right after signing in. Guild payloads list their current occupants in `voice_states`, but the decoder never read that field and the reducer started every guild with an empty table, so people appeared only after their next move or rejoin. The field is decoded (bounded at 1000 per guild) and installed by READY and GUILD_CREATE; a payload that omits it leaves what the client already knows alone, so a partial GUILD_CREATE cannot wipe the room.

## 0.1.0-alpha.6 — 2026-10-25

### Fixed
- Password login's CAPTCHA no longer hangs as an empty window when it cannot load. The window connects directly and cannot use the app's network recovery, so on a filtered route the widget never appeared and nothing explained why; the page now reports a failed provider script or a widget that is not ready after 20 seconds, the window closes, and the login screen names `discord.com` and `js.hcaptcha.com` and points at QR or token login.
- The login challenge's `captcha_session_id` is returned as `x-captcha-session-id` next to `x-captcha-key` and `x-captcha-rqtoken`, as the official client sends it; a solution without it can be refused as a repeat challenge.

## 0.1.0-alpha.5 — 2026-10-25

### Fixed
- Friends, blocks and pending friend requests that existed before sign-in are visible again. READY lists each user once and its relationship entries carry only a `user_id`, so the decoder dropped every one of them at login and only relationships changed during the session appeared — pre-existing requests looked lost. The entry's user is now resolved from READY's `users` table (the inline `user` object still wins when present), and the deduplicated `fixtures/gateway/ready.json` asserts it.

## 0.1.0-alpha.4 — 2026-10-25

### Added
- Settings → Application, Advanced gained **Allow all** and **Turn all off** next to the unverified-writes list: every write group is switched for the current session in one click instead of one checkbox at a time. It is still session-only — nothing is saved, and the next restart, log-out or account switch starts from the default groups — and the shortcut only appears while an account is signed in.

## 0.1.0-alpha.3 — 2026-10-25

### Fixed
- Auto recovery now waits only for the failed endpoint/role/TLS profile; an unrelated CDN campaign cannot stall healthy API requests. Stale demands are discarded on network changes without resetting diagnostic budgets.
- Concurrent HTTP connections use bounded, per-CONNECT attempt IDs and receipts instead of a last-result-per-host ledger. Proxy/TLS errors and destination addresses cannot cross between requests; cancellation revokes the ID, and connector timeouts retain their typed diagnostics without replaying requests.
- TCP dialing prefers a different IP family for its second attempt and gives each attempt its own deadline, so multiple AAAA answers cannot hide an available IPv4 result. The two-dial cap and proxy's ten-second overall limit remain.

- Add optional Windows packet recovery (M96): a separate, manually elevated
  WinDivert helper, PID-authenticated local IPC and exact ClientHello filters;
  packet fake/disorder and sequence-overlap profiles inspired by zapret.
  The ordinary client retains credentials and certificate verification.
  Anonymous API access was restored on the reported direct Windows route;
  authenticated login/Gateway and media evidence are recorded separately.

- Preserve typed network failures and HTTP server status codes in login errors,
  and add a debug-only `--network-check` using the actual fastcord executable
  for process-specific routing diagnostics without account credentials.

- Add separate SOCKS5 UDP ASSOCIATE media routing (M95) for voice/camera and
  Go Live, with retained control TCP, source/header/MTU validation, eight shared
  association slots and fail-closed control-loss handling. HTTP CONNECT remains
  TCP-only; proxy mode never leaks media direct. Real proxy NAT and 30-minute
  Windows voice/share interoperability gates remain pending.

- Add separately opt-in experimental UDP discovery preludes (M94): three bounded
  direct flows inside one signaling connection, per-join byte/request/deadline
  limits, retained old ports and visible variant progress. The original zero
  fixture is a laboratory candidate with unverified filtered-network effectiveness;
  the experiment remains off by default and disabled in Proxy mode.

- Add user-configured HTTP/HTTPS CONNECT and SOCKS5 TCP routing for every HTTP
  and WebSocket role (M93), remote destination DNS, endpoint-bound native proxy
  passwords, categorical failures and a pre-join TCP-only media explanation.
  Routing never silently falls back to direct; live proxy interoperability gates
  remain pending.

- Add an application-scoped socket/HTTP boundary, private authenticated CONNECT
  bridge and bounded TLS recovery catalog/selection (M92), network preferences
  and Windows network-change hints, preserving account and write retry limits.
  Blocked-network effectiveness and remaining manual gates are unverified.

### Added
- Redesigned sign-in, unified settings, conversation menus, composer and call controls; English/Russian interface selection with system-language detection and persisted preferences, plus friend-based new DM/group selection.
- WebSocket permessage-deflate: the main Gateway, remote-auth and voice WebSockets now offer Chromium's `permessage-deflate; client_max_window_bits`, negotiate the server's parameters, compress and decompress RFC 7692 messages with byte-bounded buffers, and fall back to the uncompressed transport when the server declines. The opening handshake, the accept-key/extension validation and the RFC 7692 adapter live in a new shared crate, `fastcord-ws` (no new external dependency); unsolicited or malformed extensions, a bad accept key and compression bombs are typed failures, and the offered/negotiated values still await a sanitized official capture (ADR 0005, IDENTITY_AUDIT A9).

### Changed
- The Windows executable reserves a 4 MiB main-thread stack (pages committed on demand), preventing debug GUI stack overflow when the redesigned settings and network panels are composed. Specialized network and fake-script dispatch no longer enters the large account-handler frame.
- Integrated scoped network recovery with the existing desktop identity, cookie/fingerprint handling and WebSocket compression. Network recovery is reachable before sign-in and from Application settings; it replaces the old byedpi switch while preserving legacy settings fields.
- `x-discord-locale` now reports the account's chosen client locale from READY (and a live settings change) instead of the OS locale, while `system_locale` and `accept-language` keep the OS values; the locale is shape-checked, and an account switch starts from the OS locale again (ADR 0005, IDENTITY_AUDIT B5).
- The `discord.com` cookie jar now scopes and expires cookies like the official client's browser profile: `Domain`/`Path` (host-only without `Domain`, a foreign `Domain` refused), `Max-Age`/`Expires` on the request clock, longest path first, still bounded at 32 cookies and 16 KiB in memory (IDENTITY_AUDIT A8).
- Public CDN fetches send only the profile user agent and a purpose-specific `Accept` (Chromium's image list for an image subresource, `*/*` for a file download), with no cookie, authorization, fingerprint, installation id or super properties (IDENTITY_AUDIT C2).
- The daily identity pin check now states that its five hosts are manifest coverage, and an ignored Windows test reads the Electron 42.11.15 / Chromium 148.0.7778.280 pair out of the installed stable client's own binary, so the runtime pair has recorded evidence on Windows x64 (IDENTITY_AUDIT C1, plan I2).
- Identity review: separate REST properties view excludes Gateway-only installation fields while preserving `X-Installation-ID` (profile version 4); native metadata is nullable, macOS uses its own metadata fallback and reports Darwin major as its SDK; the daily pin check covers Windows x64/ARM64, macOS x64/ARM64 and Linux x64. REST and WebSocket encoding headers include zstd, with a REST decoder; the Referer follows the visible guild/channel or DM. Merge current main into the identity branch while preserving byedpi for version fetches, QR exchange and both WebSocket transports. Official capture, cross-platform runtime-pair evidence, WS compression and valid-alt acceptance remain open.
- Message nonces now have the official client's shape: the send time in the snowflake position and the low 22 bits zero instead of random, from one process-wide sequence that moves to the next millisecond when one is already used (ADR 0005, IDENTITY_AUDIT B1).
- Requests to Discord carry the desktop client's Chromium header set (`sec-ch-ua*` from the pinned Chromium, `sec-fetch-*`, `accept-language`, `accept-encoding: gzip, deflate, br` with decoding, `referer`, `origin` on writes, `x-discord-timezone` from the OS) and a bounded in-memory `discord.com` cookie jar; the QR login exchange now sends the identity headers too, and the Gateway, QR and voice WebSocket handshakes carry the user agent, language and no-cache headers (ADR 0005, IDENTITY_AUDIT A7-A9). New dependencies: reqwest's gzip/deflate/brotli decoders.
- REST and the Gateway use API version 9, the official client's (`/api/v9`, `?v=9`), instead of 10; the fake server follows (ADR 0005, IDENTITY_AUDIT A6).
- Password login sends `X-Fingerprint`, obtained once per process from Discord's unauthenticated `GET /experiments`, as the official client does before it has a token (ADR 0005, IDENTITY_AUDIT A5).
- The client analytics heartbeat session in the identity is regenerated every 30 minutes, and the installation id Discord issues in READY (`apex_experiments`) is kept in an `installation-id` file of the data folder and sent as `installation_id` and `X-Installation-ID` from then on (ADR 0005, IDENTITY_AUDIT A2-A4). The REST client and the Gateway now share one `Identity`; `Gateway::start` takes it from the REST client.
- The Gateway Identify and `x-super-properties` now carry the official Discord desktop (Electron) profile (ADR 0005, `PROFILE_VERSION` 3): `browser: "Discord Client"`, `client_version` and `native_build_number` from Discord's public update manifest and `client_build_number` from `/app` (fetched once per process, bundled fallbacks), the Electron 42.11.15 / Chromium 148.0.7778.280 user agent and `browser_version`, the host's full `os_version`, `os_arch`/`app_arch`, the Windows `os_sdk_version` and the Linux `window_manager`/`distro` (`fastcord_platform::os_release`), a launch signature with the 12 client-mod bits cleared and a per-launch `client_heartbeat_session_id`; `device`, `referrer*` and `client_app_state` are no longer sent. Reception reports, REMB and sink wants drive bounded bitrate changes, keyframe requests and pause/resume `VoiceEvent`s. The media crate never decodes/renders; M28 connects the app's watch decoder and renderer, while capture/send remains M29. Independent RFC 6184/4588 + AEAD vectors and real MLS/DAVE loss/reorder/RTX driver tests cover transport; official-client interoperability remains pending.
- AV1 receive (M26: `fastcord-media::video::av1`, the voice driver, `fastcord-video::av1`, and app stream workers): AV1 is advertised decode-only with payload type 101/RTX 102; the bounded AOM RTP depacketizer handles aggregation, fragmented OBU headers/lengths, strict malformed input, N-keyframe gating, RTX/loss repair, and per-SSRC codec changes. DAVE seals complete temporal units before fragmentation, preserving authenticated OBU headers and non-final lengths; sizes are restored only after decryption. dav1d 1.5.4 is statically built from a pinned, hash-verified source archive with the target C compiler (portable C, no assembler, network, DLL, or FFmpeg); decoding is lazy, input capped at 2 MiB, native pictures at 4096 per side/4096x2304 pixels and 256 MiB retained allocation. Borrowed 8-bit luma avoids a copy; other supported YUV layouts/depths convert to NV12, RGB/identity streams are refused, and failed native contexts are discarded. Independent RTP/AEAD vectors, genuine libaom streams checked against its reference decoder, malformed/damage sweeps, real MLS/DAVE driver tests, and bounded threaded-decoder tests cover the path. Official-client AV1 interoperability remains pending (U4).
- Video playback in iced (M28): explicit Watch/Show/Stop controls for Go Live offers in the parent voice channel; a latest-frame lease slot and coalesced, visibility-only subscription, with no fixed redraw timer or video pixels in UI messages. The dedicated worker now decodes H.264 through Media Foundation/VideoToolbox as well as AV1 through dav1d; hiding the pane, focus loss/minimize, or an occluding view suspends decode and releases references, and resume requests a fresh keyframe. Three recyclable decoded buffers are capped at 48 MiB of owned capacities; wgpu reuses one Y/UV texture pair and converts NV12 with preserved color matrix/range and sample aspect ratio. The no-adapter tiny-skia path converts directly to leased BGRA off the UI thread and blends borrowed pixmaps without per-frame image encoding/handles. Stop watching, logout, stream end, and parent-voice changes release playback state without replacing the parent voice connection. Deterministic logic/metadata tests and real native fixture-window checks are documented in `docs/TESTING.md`; live official-stream acceptance remains pending.
- One web-client identity for the whole run (ADR 0005): the profile is built once at login — `client_build_number` fetched from discord.com web assets there rather than at every Gateway start, plus the per-launch identity (`client_launch_id` and `launch_signature` are two fresh UUIDv4s, `client_app_state` is `"focused"`) — and is reused unchanged by every Identify and every reconnect. The same profile now identifies the REST client: `x-super-properties` carries the identical properties JSON base64-encoded, alongside its user agent, `x-discord-locale`, and the client fingerprint constant, so the Gateway and API halves of a session never disagree about who the client is. The credential-free CDN client is untouched and still sends none of these. The launch identity is generated once per launch, never per Identify; the desktop-only `native_build_number` is still not sent.
- Windows screen share (M29): the Go Live row offers **Share screen** while joined to a guild voice channel. A picker lists the machine's screens and the shareable top-level windows (visible, titled, not minimized or suspended, never fastcord's own); choosing one starts Windows.Graphics.Capture on a dedicated owner thread first, and only when the OS accepted it is the stream created (opcode 18), so a refused permission or a vanished window never creates a stream. Pictures are scaled to fit 1280×720 (never upscaled, even edges) and converted to BT.709 NV12 by a D3D11 video processor (CPU converter fallback), paced to 30 fps on capture timestamps, and encoded by Media Foundation (hardware first, Microsoft's software transform as the fallback, falling back to software if a hardware encoder fails while running). The stream's own voice connection offers one H.264 screen layer; nothing is encoded until the server selected H.264 for us, keyframes follow PLI/FIR (answered immediately even for a static screen, which is also resent about once a second), the encoder bitrate follows the transport's congestion feedback and the server's sink wants (a paused layer slows capture to one picture a second), and a refused unit restarts the stream at a throttled keyframe. Resizing a shared window reopens the encoder only after the new size held for 300 ms; closing the source, a permission refusal, an encoder failure, a stream failure, STREAM_DELETE (with Discord's reason), Stop sharing, and leaving or moving the voice channel all stop the capture thread (joined, bounded) and delete the stream when it exists, without touching the parent voice connection. The capture border and cursor stay at the OS defaults. Other platforms report that capture is not implemented yet. Hardware tests on Windows (AMD Radeon RX 9070 XT, Microsoft software encoder) captured a real window and display, round-tripped the encoded units through the decoder and compared the painted colors; official-client viewing at 720p30 and microphone continuity during a share remain live acceptance (Refs #29).
